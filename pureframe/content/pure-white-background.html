Why a white backdrop photographs grey

A camera exposes for the product, not the backdrop. White seamless paper or a light tent lit at the same level as the product records as light grey, typically around 230–250. Your eye and your screen call that white, but an automated check that samples background pixels sees grey. Shadows on the sweep, light fall-off toward the corners and a slight colour cast from mixed lighting make it worse.

How to check for true white

  • PureFrame: drop the photo in. It reports what share of the background is exactly #FFFFFF and the average backdrop colour.
  • Photoshop: open the Info panel and hover the backdrop in several places, especially the corners. Every reading should be 255, 255, 255.
  • The threshold trick: add a Threshold adjustment layer set to 254. Anything that isn't pure white turns black, so a grey backdrop lights up instantly. Delete the layer when you're done.

Fix 1: Get it right in camera

Light the backdrop separately and expose it about one stop brighter than the product. The background then clips to white while the product stays correctly exposed. Don't overdo it: a backdrop that's far too bright spills light around the edges of the product and washes out its outline. Keep the product a few feet in front of the backdrop so its light doesn't wrap around.

Fix 2: Clean it up in Photoshop

  1. Levels (Ctrl/Cmd + L): click the white-point eyedropper on the backdrop. This also neutralises colour casts, but it brightens the product too, so check highlights on white or shiny products.
  2. Select and fill: Select › Subject, refine the edge, invert the selection and fill with white. Keep a soft shadow layer if you want the product grounded.
  3. Run the threshold check again before exporting.

White products on white

White plates, mugs and appliances are the hard case. Give the product a defined edge: a darker rim, a soft shadow, or a gradient across the surface. If an automatic cleanup eats into the product's edge, lower the cleanup strength (in PureFrame: Fix settings › Background cleanup). Only backdrop connected to the outside of the photo is whitened, so the inside of a plate keeps its tone.
